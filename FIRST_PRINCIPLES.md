# Universal Observatory Operating System
## First Principles v0.1

Status: FOUNDATIONAL / OBSERVATIONAL / BOUNDARY-FIRST  
Authority: NONE  
UNKNOWN → HOLD

---

## Principle 1 — Reality Precedes Observation

Reality exists before it is observed.

Reality → Observation → Measurement → Evidence → Interpretation → Explanation → Verification

Nothing in the Observatory may reverse this order.

---

## Principle 2 — Observation Is Not Reality

Observation is a bounded contact with reality.

Observation is constrained by:

- instrument
- resolution
- calibration
- environment
- timing
- uncertainty
- interpretation boundary

Observation ≠ Reality

---

## Principle 3 — Evidence Precedes Explanation

Signals become evidence before they become explanation.

Signal → Measurement → Evidence → Comparison → Pressure Test → Explanation

Evidence may exist without explanation.

---

## Principle 4 — Every Observation Has Lineage

Every observation must preserve its path:

Reality → Object → Instrument → Signal → Measurement → Evidence → Interpretation → Remaining Unknown

No observation is lineage-free.

---

## Principle 5 — Unknown Is a Valid State

Unknown is not failure.

Unknown may mean:

- not measured
- not observable yet
- insufficient evidence
- conflicting evidence
- requires better instrument
- requires better theory
- requires replication

UNKNOWN → HOLD

---

## Principle 6 — Boundaries Are First-Class Objects

Every object, observation, claim, model, and recovery has boundaries.

Boundary types include:

- observation boundary
- measurement boundary
- calibration boundary
- evidence boundary
- interpretation boundary
- simulation boundary
- prediction boundary
- execution boundary
- authority boundary
- recovery boundary
- admissibility boundary
- governability boundary

---

## Principle 7 — Every Claim Has Status

No claim is statusless.

Allowed status examples:

- observed
- measured
- replicated
- corroborated
- model-supported
- simulation-supported
- candidate
- conflicting
- unknown
- retired

---

## Principle 8 — Failure Produces Knowledge

Failure is an observation.

Expectation → Observation → Deviation → Failure → Localization → Recovery → Updated Boundary

Failures must be preserved, not erased.

---

## Principle 9 — Recovery Is Observable

Recovery must be inspected.

Recovered ≠ Restored  
Functional Recovery ≠ Possibility Recovery  
Recovery ≠ Future Optionality

---

## Principle 10 — Questions Are First-Class Objects

Questions are preserved as objects.

Each question may contain:

- current evidence
- unknowns
- candidate instruments
- competing explanations
- priority
- status

---

## Principle 11 — Scale Is Continuous

Every object belongs somewhere in scale.

Universe → Cosmic Web → Galaxy → Star → Planet → Region → Rock → Crystal → Cell → Molecule → Atom → Particle

---

## Principle 12 — Observation Is Multi-Perspective

No single instrument owns reality.

Multiple instruments may observe the same object through different signals.

Agreement increases confidence.  
Conflict increases inspection need.

---

## Principle 13 — Scientific Memory Matters

The Observatory preserves:

- revisions
- failed attempts
- negative results
- superseded models
- historical context
- uncertainty changes

---

## Principle 14 — The Observatory Observes Itself

The system must track:

- coverage
- missing domains
- missing instruments
- unknown growth
- evidence gaps
- boundary health
- data freshness
- reproducibility status

## Mission

To organize observational capabilities, scientific evidence, instruments, models, boundaries, failures, recoveries, and unanswered questions into a transparent cross-disciplinary observability platform that preserves the distinction between observation, inference, explanation, verification, and uncertainty.
