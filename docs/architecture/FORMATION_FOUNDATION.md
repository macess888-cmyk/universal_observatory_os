# Purpose

This document introduces Formation as the next architectural layer of the Observatory.

The Observable Foundation preserves observable structure.

The Structural Substrate validates observable structure.

Formation introduces the inspection of observable structures as coherent wholes.

A Formation is not a replacement for individual observables.

A Formation is a higher-order composition built from them.

---

# Candidate Definition

A Formation is an inspectable composition of observable objects and their structural relationships.

A Formation may contain:

- observable objects
- observable relationships
- observable evidence
- observable events
- observable timelines
- observable graph structure

A Formation is inspected as one coherent structure.

# Structural Questions

Formation inspection asks questions that individual validators cannot.

Examples:

Is the structure internally coherent?

Are important observable components missing?

Are observable regions disconnected?

Does supporting evidence exist?

Are observable timelines incomplete?

Can the observable graph still be traversed?

Where are the observable unknowns?

---

# Formation Boundary

Formation inspection does not establish:

- truth
- correctness
- explanation
- prediction
- authority
- causation

Formation preserves inspectable structure only.

# Responsibilities

Formation is responsible for:

- composing structural observations
- exposing structural gaps
- preserving unknowns
- supporting higher-order inspection
- enabling future topology

Formation is not responsible for:

- making decisions
- drawing conclusions
- replacing human judgment
- determining scientific validity
