# Purpose

This document defines the foundational observable entities of the Universal Observatory Operating System.

The Observatory preserves observable structure.

It does not preserve authority.

It does not preserve explanation.

It does not preserve proof.

Observable entities provide stable, inspectable representations of recorded observations.

---

# Core Principle

The Observatory records observable structure.

It intentionally separates:

Observation

↓

Representation

↓

Interpretation

↓

Explanation

↓

Proof

Each layer has different responsibilities.

Only the first two belong to the kernel.

---

# Observable

An Observable is the fundamental architectural unit of the Observatory.

Every observable:

- possesses a stable identity
- can be registered
- may participate in relationships
- may appear on a timeline
- may be inspected
- may contain metadata
- may preserve unknowns

Being observable does not imply:

- correctness
- authority
- truth
- causation
- explanation
- proof

---

# First-Class Observable Entities

The current Observatory recognizes four foundational observable entities.

## ObservableObject

Represents:

Something that exists within the Observatory.

Question answered:

What exists?

---

## ObservableRelationship

Represents:

A recorded connection between observable entities.

Question answered:

What is connected?

Relationships do not imply:

- causation
- correctness
- evidence
- authority

---

## ObservableEvent

Represents:

A recorded occurrence or change.

Question answered:

What changed?

Events preserve history.

They do not explain why change occurred.

---

## ObservableEvidence

Represents:

Recorded support associated with an observation.

Question answered:

What supports the recorded observation?

Evidence may:

- support
- contradict
- contextualize
- question

Evidence does not imply:

- proof
- authority
- certainty
- explanation

# Architectural Boundary

The Observable Foundation defines observable structure.

It intentionally does not define:

scientific theories

business logic

governance

reasoning

decision making

prediction

domain-specific interpretation

Those belong in services, plugins, or higher-level research systems.

---

# Design Principles

Preserve structure before explanation.

Preserve identity before interpretation.

Relationships are recorded.

Meaning is investigated.

Evidence supports observation.

Unknowns remain visible.

Architecture should emerge through repeated observation rather than anticipation.
