# OBSERVATION

A recorded interaction between reality and an observing system.

Observation does not imply explanation.

---

# SIGNAL

A detectable manifestation produced through interaction with reality.

Examples:

• photons
• radio waves
• gravitational waves
• particles
• chemical signatures
• pressure
• temperature
• electrical activity

---

# MEASUREMENT

The quantified characterization of an observed signal.

---

# EVIDENCE

Measurements and observations preserved with provenance and context.

Evidence supports inspection.

Evidence does not automatically establish explanation.

---

Anything capable of being observed, measured, related, or investigated.

# OBSERVATORY

A bounded environment that preserves observations, evidence, relationships, and uncertainty.

---

# BOUNDARY

An explicit limit describing where an observation, measurement, model, or claim remains valid.

---

# MODEL

An explanatory representation consistent with available evidence.

Models remain revisable.

---

# THEORY

A coherent explanatory framework supported by extensive evidence.

Theories remain inspectable.

---

# HYPOTHESIS

A testable candidate explanation.

---

# SIMULATION

A computational representation used to explore possible system behavior.

Simulation ≠ Observation

---

# UNKNOWN

Information not currently established.

Unknown is a valid state.

UNKNOWN → HOLD

---

# FAILURE

An observed deviation from expected behavior.

Failures are observational data.

---

# RECOVERY

The restoration of capability following degradation or failure.

Recovery ≠ Restoration of Possibility

---

# OPTIONALITY

The remaining set of reachable future states available to a system.

---

# ADMISSIBILITY

The conditions under which execution is considered acceptable before action occurs.

Execution ≠ Admissibility

---

# GOVERNABILITY

The preserved capacity to influence future trajectories through available corrective action.

Capability ≠ Governability

---

# PROVENANCE

The documented origin and history of an observation, dataset, or object.

---

# CONFIDENCE

A characterization of how strongly available evidence supports a claim.

Confidence is not certainty.

---

# UNCERTAINTY

The remaining incompleteness associated with an observation, measurement, model, or explanation.

Uncertainty should be preserved.

---

# RELATIONSHIP

A defined connection between two or more ObservableObjects.

Relationships are first-class objects.

The universal parent object from which every entity within the Observatory inherits.
